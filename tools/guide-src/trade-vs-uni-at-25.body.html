On the 2026–27 rules, a school leaver who becomes an electrician is usually well ahead of a business graduate at 25 — even when we pay the tradie only the award minimum. By 35 the gap is much smaller, and small changes in assumptions can flip it. Here's the working.

The two people

Jordan leaves school at 17 after Year 11 and starts a four-year electrical apprenticeship. We pay Jordan the award minimum every year, then the qualified electrician's award minimum of $1,223.11 a week, rising 3% a year. Jordan's job is in housing, so Jordan gets the $10,000 Key Apprenticeship Program payments.

Sam finishes Year 12 at 18 and does a three-year business degree. Sam earns $15,000 a year from part-time work while finishing school and studying, borrows the 2026 maximum student contribution for business ($17,399 a year) through HECS-HELP, then starts on the median graduate salary for business and management of $74,000, also rising 3% a year. Sam's HELP debt is indexed at 2.8% a year (the rate applied on 1 June 2026) and repaid under the 2026–27 marginal repayment system.

The $15,000 part-time income and the 3% pay rises are our assumptions, not official figures — you can change both in the calculator.

Jordan is ahead by about $133,366. Three things drive that: Jordan earns a full-time wage for the four years Sam spends finishing school and studying, Jordan's KAP payments add $10,000, and Sam is carrying a HELP debt that's still bigger than the total he borrowed, because indexation outpaced Sam's early repayments.

What happens by 35

Sam's salary starts higher and grows from a bigger base, so the gap narrows every year after graduation. Keep the same assumptions and run them to 35: Jordan's net position is $1,033,309 and Sam's is $978,578, so Jordan is still ahead by about $54,731 — and Sam still owes $30,278 in HELP.

Change Sam to a four-year engineering degree (a $9,537 yearly contribution and the $82,500 engineering median salary) and the gap at 35 shrinks to about $7,759. That's close enough that a slightly bigger graduate pay rise would put the engineer in front.

Now go the other way. Many qualified electricians earn well above the award minimum through overtime, above-award rates or running their own business. If Jordan earns 20% above award once qualified, Jordan is ahead by about $202,461 at 35.

Why we're careful with this comparison

  • We compare an award minimum with a median. Sam's graduate salary is the typical (median) salary from the 2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey; Jordan's qualified wage is the legal minimum. That's a cautious choice for the trade side.
  • Graduates don't all land full-time jobs straight away. In the same survey, 75.4% of domestic undergraduates were in full-time work four to six months after finishing.
  • Graduate pay often grows faster than 3% in the early years. If you expect that, raise the pay rise and watch the uni line catch up sooner.
  • Not everyone finishes. Around 4 in 10 trade apprentices drop out within four years (see our guide on completion), and plenty of students change or drop courses too.
  • We hold 2026–27 tax and HELP thresholds constant, which slightly overstates later tax and repayments. We also index each year's new HELP debt straight away, whereas real debt under 11 months old isn't indexed on the next 1 June — so Sam's balance is a little overstated. Super, living costs and house prices aren't in the model.

So which should you pick?

The numbers say a trade is a genuinely strong financial start, and it's no worse than many degrees over a decade. They don't say which work you'll enjoy, which suits your body over 40 years, or which opens the doors you care about. Frequently asked questions

Does HELP debt grow even while I'm studying?
Yes. Debt is indexed every 1 June. The rate applied on 1 June 2026 was 2.8%. You only make compulsory repayments once your income is above the threshold ($69,528 in 2026–27).
How much HELP do I repay on a graduate salary?
Under the 2026–27 system you repay 15 cents for every dollar of repayment income above $69,528, up to $129,717. On $74,000 that's $670.80 for the year.
Do apprentices pay anything like HELP?
Not for the apprenticeship itself. Some apprentices pay TAFE fees (often low or free in NSW), and the optional Australian Apprenticeship Support Loan works a bit like HELP.
Is a trade or uni better for earning potential?
It depends on the trade, the degree and the person. On median graduate salaries and award-minimum trade wages, the trade path is ahead at 25 and the gap narrows a lot by 35; a higher-paid degree like engineering is close to level by then.

If you start a housing-construction apprenticeship, the federal government will pay you up to $10,000 on top of your wages, in five instalments over the apprenticeship. Your employer may get up to $5,000 too. Here's who qualifies, when the money lands, and the catches worth knowing.

What is the Key Apprenticeship Program?

The Key Apprenticeship Program (KAP) is part of the Australian Apprenticeships Incentive System run by the Department of Employment and Workplace Relations (DEWR). It's aimed at the trades Australia needs to hit its housing and clean-energy targets. For apprentices it started on 1 July 2025; the matching employer incentive started on 1 January 2026.

How much, and when

A full-time apprentice can receive up to $10,000, paid as five instalments of $2,000:

  1. After 6 months$2,000
  2. After 12 months$2,000
  3. After 24 months$2,000
  4. After 36 months$2,000
  5. On completion$2,000

Part-time apprentices can receive up to $5,000, as five instalments of $1,000 at the same points.

Notice the gap: there's no payment at 18 months, and the last $2,000 only comes when you finish. The program is deliberately weighted to reward people who stick with it.

Who qualifies?

In plain terms, DEWR's rules say you need to:

  • have started (or restarted) your apprenticeship on or after 1 July 2025;
  • be doing a Certificate III or higher qualification that's on the Australian Apprenticeships Priority List and identified as a housing construction or new energy qualification;
  • work for an employer who can give you meaningful work and experience in that sector — both of you sign a declaration saying so;
  • meet the general eligibility rules for apprenticeship incentives (your Apprentice Connect Australia provider checks these).

Lists published by Apprentice Connect providers put the housing stream at around 21 occupations, including carpenter, carpenter and joiner, bricklayer, wall and floor tiler, painter, plasterer, roof tiler, stonemason and cabinet maker. Electricians and plumbers are listed under both the housing and new-energy streams.† DEWR doesn't publish a simple list we could check directly, so confirm your occupation with your provider before you count on the money.

What employers get

Employers of eligible apprentices who started on or after 1 January 2026 can get the KAP Employer Incentive: up to $5,000 for a full-time apprentice ($2,000 at 6 months and $3,000 at 12 months), or up to $2,500 part-time. The Minister has said this employer payment is available for apprentices who start by December 2026, so check what applies if you're hiring in 2027.

Employers of apprentices in other Priority List occupations (not KAP) can instead get the Priority Hiring Incentive — up to $2,500 in the first year for apprentices starting from 1 January 2026. Our employer cost calculator shows what either incentive does to the real weekly cost.

If you're not in a KAP trade

Apprentices in other Priority List occupations may get the Australian Apprentice Training Support Payment instead: up to $2,500 for full-time apprentices, paid as $625 at 6, 12, 18 and 24 months. You can't get both. If your employer can no longer give you housing or new-energy work, DEWR's rules move you to this payment and your employer to the Priority Hiring Incentive.

Other help worth knowing about

  • Living Away From Home Allowance: if you have to move out of your family home for the apprenticeship, you may get $125.52 a week in year one, $94.14 in year two and $47.07 in year three.
  • Australian Apprenticeship Support Loan: an interest-free loan of up to $27,048 (2026–27) over a four-year apprenticeship, repaid through the tax system once your income reaches $69,528. Finish your apprenticeship and the balance is cut by 20%.

How to claim

You don't apply on a government website yourself. When your training contract is signed, your Apprentice Connect Australia provider (the free service that registers apprenticeships) checks eligibility, organises the declarations and tells you how each instalment is claimed. Keep your contact and bank details up to date with them, and stay enrolled and progressing with your training — payments depend on it.

Frequently asked questions

Is the $10,000 paid on top of my wages?
Yes. It's paid to you separately from your wages, and your employer still has to pay you at least the award minimum.
Do I pay tax on KAP payments?
Our calculators don't add KAP payments to taxable income. Check how they're treated for your situation with the ATO or your Apprentice Connect provider.
I started my apprenticeship before 1 July 2025. Can I get it?
The apprentice payment is for apprenticeships that commenced or recommenced on or after 1 July 2025. Ask your Apprentice Connect provider what applies to your contract.
What happens if I change employers?
Tell your Apprentice Connect provider. If your new employer can't provide housing-construction or new-energy work, you may move to the training support payment instead.

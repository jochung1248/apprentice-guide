01 · The gap

You earn $32.
They bill $115.

The award minimum for a qualified electrician works out to about $32 an hour. A Sydney sparky typically charges a customer around $115 an hour including GST. Most qualified tradies earn above the award, but the gap is still big. Here’s where it goes.

Out of $115 an hourWho keeps it
GST (1/11th of the price)ATO · $10.45
Your wage, super and leaveYou
Insurance, licences, ute, fuel, toolsRunning costs
Quotes, admin, travel, quiet weeksUnbilled time
What’s leftProfit → owner

As an employee, the profit goes to the business owner. When you own the business, you keep it. You also take on the costs and the risk.

The catch

Not every hour is billable. Quoting, driving, buying materials and chasing invoices are unpaid. A rate that looks huge can shrink fast. Page 4 shows how to work out yours.

Charge-out figure: typical Sydney range from whatsthedamage.com.au (a cost guide, not official data). Award: Electrical, Electronic and Communications Contracting Award, $1,223.11 a week ÷ 38 hours.

02 · Getting set up

The first
steps

  • Get an ABN. It’s free through the Australian Business Register. Without one, businesses paying you must withhold 47% tax. Watch for sites that charge for this.
  • Pick a structure. Sole trader is the simplest and cheapest. A company costs more to run but can pay a 25% tax rate on profits kept in it. Ask an accountant which fits.
  • Register for GST when you need to. You must register within 21 days of your turnover reaching $75,000 a year. Registering is free.
  • Check your licence. Most trades need a contractor licence from your state’s regulator before you can contract work in your own name.
  • Get insured. Public liability at minimum. Consider income protection, because nobody pays you when you’re sick.
  • Open a separate business bank account. Keeps tax time simple.
  • Set up bookkeeping software for quotes, invoices and BAS.
  • Register a business name if you trade under anything other than your own name.
Tip: you can apply for your ABN and register for GST in the same online application.

03 · Pricing

Work out
your rate

Start from what you need to earn, not what the bloke down the road charges. Here’s the method, with example numbers only.

StepExample
1. The pay you want, plus 12% for your own super$100,000 + $12,000
2. Add yearly running costs (ute, insurance, tools, phone, software, licences)+ $40,000
3. Total you need to bring in$152,000
4. Billable hours: 46 weeks × 30 billable hours (the rest is quoting, travel, admin)1,380 hrs
5. Break-even rate (before profit and GST)$110 / hr

Then add a margin for profit and quiet months, and add GST on top if you’re registered. Swap in your own numbers. Your accountant can sanity-check them.

Three rules that protect your rate

  • Charge a call-out or minimum fee so short jobs still pay.
  • Mark up materials. You carry the cost until you’re paid.
  • Quote in writing with clear terms, including what’s not included.

04 · Owner tax perks

Four perks
owners get

$20,000
Instant asset write-off

Businesses with turnover under $10M can deduct each asset costing under $20,000 straight away, instead of over several years. Made permanent from 1 July 2026. It helps cash flow; the tool still costs what it costs.

$1,000
Small business tax offset

Sole traders and partners in businesses with turnover under $5M get 16% off the tax on their business income, up to $1,000 a year. Worked out automatically from your tax return.

$32,500
Deductible super

No one pays super for you, but you can pay your own and claim a deduction. The concessional cap is $32,500 for 2026–27, taxed at 15% inside super. Lodge a notice of intent with your fund first.

$0 CGT
Sell the business

Small business CGT concessions can cut or remove tax when you sell. Owned it 15+ years and retiring at 55 or older? The gain can be fully exempt. Others include a 50% reduction and a $500,000 retirement exemption.

Plus: run it as a company and profits kept in the company are taxed at 25% (base rate entity). You pay personal tax when you take the money out. Structure matters, so get advice before you choose.

All four have conditions (turnover limits, asset tests, age tests). Check ato.gov.au or ask a registered tax agent.

05 · Cash flow

Cash is
king

Profitable businesses still go broke when the money comes in later than the bills go out. Late payment is common: Xero data shows small businesses were paid on average 6.6 days late in the December 2025 quarter. The Small Business Ombudsman found 23% of big businesses take more than 120 days to pay small suppliers.

  • Take a deposit before you buy materials on bigger jobs.
  • Invoice the same day you finish. Phone apps make this easy.
  • Short terms. 7 days for homeowners; get progress payments on long jobs.
  • Make paying easy: card on the spot, bank details on every invoice.
  • Follow up on day one of being overdue, not week three.
  • Keep a buffer of two or three months’ running costs.
Tax-time rule

The GST you collect isn’t yours. Neither is the tax on your profit. Move a set slice of every payment into a separate “tax” account so the BAS and tax bill never catch you short. Ask your accountant what percentage to use.

Boss chases unpaid invoicesYou chase them
Clock off at knock-offQuotes and admin at night

Construction also has more insolvencies than almost any other industry. Good work isn’t enough on its own; pricing (page 4) and cash flow (page 6) matter just as much.

Still worth it?

For plenty of people, yes. Construction is Australia’s largest industry by number of businesses: 454,850 of them. In 2024–25 there were 76,414 new construction businesses, more than any other industry. Many started with one tradie and one ute.

A sensible path: finish your apprenticeship, get a few years as a qualified tradie, learn how your boss quotes and runs jobs, then go out on your own with a buffer saved.

07 · Before you quit your job

The checklist

  • Qualified, and licensed to contract in my state
  • Two to three months of running costs saved
  • My break-even hourly rate worked out
  • ABN, structure decided, GST plan
  • Public liability (and income protection) quoted
  • Accountant or registered tax agent lined up
  • Bookkeeping app, business account, tax account
  • First few customers or a subcontract lined up

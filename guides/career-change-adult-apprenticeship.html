Thinking of a trade at 28? A career-changer's guide to adult apprenticeships

Published 17 November 2026 · Figures checked 17 November 2026 · 4 min read

If you're in your late twenties and eyeing a trade, the biggest worry is usually money: can you afford to go back to an apprentice wage? The good news is that adult apprentices are paid far more than school leavers. Here's what to expect, and how to make the numbers work.

What counts as an adult apprentice?

Under the awards covering the building, electrical and plumbing trades, you're an adult apprentice if you're 21 or older when you start. Adult apprentices get a higher minimum rate than junior apprentices from day one.

What adult apprentices earn in 2026–27

These are full-time award minimums from 1 July 2026, with our estimate of weekly take-home pay after tax and Medicare levy.

To put that in perspective, a first-year adult carpentry apprentice earns about $57,639 a year before tax ($1,108.44 × 52). That's a pay cut for many office workers, but it's a living wage — and you're paid while you train, with no student debt for the apprenticeship itself.

Money on top of wages

  • Key Apprenticeship Program: age doesn't matter. If your apprenticeship is in an eligible housing-construction trade and started on or after 1 July 2025, you can receive up to $10,000 full-time, paid as $2,000 at 6, 12, 24 and 36 months and on completion. See our KAP guide.
  • Australian Apprenticeship Support Loan: an interest-free loan of up to $27,048 (2026–27) over a four-year apprenticeship, to help with living costs. You start repaying through the tax system once your income reaches $69,528, and finishing your apprenticeship cuts the balance by 20%.
  • Living Away From Home Allowance: only if you have to move out of a parent's or guardian's home for the first time — so it won't apply to most career changers.

Shorten the apprenticeship if you can

Most trade apprenticeships take about four years, but adults often bring skills that count. Ask your training provider about recognition of prior learning (RPL): if you've done relevant work — say, as a labourer, handyman or in a related trade — you may be able to skip some units and finish sooner. Each year you cut is a year sooner on a qualified wage.

Budgeting for the switch

  1. Work out your real take-home on an adult apprentice wage with our pay calculator, then compare it with your current spending, not your current salary.
  2. Build a buffer. A few months of expenses in the bank makes the first year far less stressful.
  3. Talk to your lender if you have a mortgage. Some lenders treat apprentice income differently; it's better to know before you resign.
  4. Count the costs of tools, boots and getting to site. Some employers cover tools; ask.
  5. Ask about above-award pay. Employers often value mature apprentices — you turn up, you've worked before, you can drive. That's a reasonable basis to negotiate.

Frequently asked questions

Am I too old to start an apprenticeship?
No. There's no upper age limit, and the awards pay adult apprentices a higher minimum because of it.
Do adult apprentices get the $10,000 KAP payment?
Yes, if the apprenticeship is in an eligible housing-construction or new-energy occupation and started on or after 1 July 2025. Age isn't a factor.
Can I do an apprenticeship part-time?
Some employers and trades allow part-time apprenticeships. They take longer, and KAP payments are halved to up to $5,000.
Who signs me up?
Once you've found an employer, an Apprentice Connect Australia provider registers your training contract and checks which payments you're eligible for. It's free.
